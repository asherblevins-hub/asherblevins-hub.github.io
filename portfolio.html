Portfolio Page

King Tut

My most recent completed game, King Tut, was created the second time I entered Chillennium, with a 3D Technical Artist I knew, and an Unreal Engine dev. For this jam I focused entirely on design for the first half: level layouts, player / game mechanics, game feel, win condition, etc. Due the nature of our time crunch, I determined a level-based design would work best, as we could continue adding levels as we had time. This was certainly the most impressive & successful game I had a part in making, staying at the top of the “most played” until after the jam had ended.

Dimensional Duel

Following the failure of BOOM-erang, I teamed up with a girl I had come to know that year for the next TAGD Fall jam. For this game, I wrote it out more clearly, with a more simple idea. Dimensional Duel: 2 guys punch/kick each other in a room. There are, of course, some gimmicks beyond that, but keeping the main idea simpler helped us to create a somewhat janky but still functional final product. Due to class load, both of us only used the three days leading up to submission for the development of this project.

BOOM-erang

My next idea BOOM-erang, made for the TAGD Spring jam, was the first I wrote up a document for. This idea suffered from the attempt to create it in an experimental python engine, on my own, with not too much development experience. I have, however, recently restarted development in Godot, as I really believe in this idea.

Last Light

The next game I worked on was a project my family made over spring break called Last Light. I took the 3D modeling I learned from Chillennium, and tried to do more complex things, like a procedural brick path, a brick maze, an island with a lighthouse on it, all more intensive subjects. And while that was an amazing experience that I enjoyed, it didn’t feel as fun as some of the other games I had worked on.

Love is Blind

My next project, Love is Blind, was my first group project: working with the president of the local game development club, a sound artist, and my brother. This time, we entered the jam at the start. But… instead of it being the same semester-long game jam, it was a 48hr in-person game jam called Chillennium. Wanting to branch out, and being intrigued by 3D software, I took the opportunity to learn Blender, a 3D modeling tool, and created all of the 3D assets for that game.

Free Bozo

The first game I ever worked on was a small project called Free Bozo for the TAGD Fall jam, that my brother and I made the night before submissions. I had known the jam was occurring, but only decided I wanted to participate at the last moment, and my brother supported me every step of the way. I barely knew how to code yet, and had no experience with specifics in design, so after we collaborated on the overall idea -- a clown escaping a high-security prison -- I worked on the amazing art assets, and my brother coded the game using C# in Unity.
